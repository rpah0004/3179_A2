School Types Across Australia

Where are Australia's schools, who runs them, and how is this changing?

Australia's schools vary across states, sectors and levels of remoteness. This visualisation looks at where schools are, which sector runs them, how big they are, and how this has changed from 2008 to 2025.

1

Where are Australia's schools?

Schools are spread across the country, but most are along the east and south-east coast.

Key observations

Each dot is one of Australia's 11,039 school locations in 2025, coloured by sector.

  • Schools cluster along the east and south-east coast, especially around the capital cities.
  • 3 in 4 schools are in NSW, Victoria or Queensland.
  • Inland and across the north, schools are sparse and far apart.
  • Government schools make up about two thirds of all schools and are found almost everywhere, including the most isolated areas.

Hover over a dot to see:

  • School name
  • Suburb and state
  • Sector
  • School type
2

Who runs Australia's schools?

Government, Catholic and Independent schools differ in how many there are, what type they are and how big they are.

3

Does location change the school landscape?

The mix and size of schools changes between major cities and remote areas.

Key observations

  • 56% of schools are in major cities. Only about 6% are in remote or very remote areas.
  • Most remote and very remote schools are in the NT, Queensland and WA.
  • The further from a city, the more likely a school is government run: about 60% in major cities, rising to about 80% in remote areas.
  • Remote schools are much smaller. The median school in a major city has 432 students, compared with about 50 in very remote areas.

How has the school landscape changed over time?

The number of schools and each sector's share of students have shifted between 2008 and 2025.
